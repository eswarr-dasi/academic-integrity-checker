How it works

Three steps, none of them on a server

The Python scoring engine is compiled to WebAssembly and run inside your tab. There is no upload, no queue and no stored copy of your draft.

1

Paste your draft

Essay, literature review, reflection, report or application statement. Quotations, citations and reference lists are detected and set aside so correctly sourced material does not distort the score.

2

The engine measures style

The text is split into equal windows of about 150 words. Each window is measured for sentence length variation, vocabulary variety, share of words used only once, punctuation variety and transition phrase rate.

3

Read the band, not the digit

The document index is the word weighted mean of the window scores, reported with a confidence interval and a band. Passages are listed so you can see exactly where the signal came from.

What the AI-writing index means

These are the actual thresholds in the engine. The unclear band is wide on purpose: in that range the evidence genuinely does not separate careful human writing from edited AI writing.

IndexBandWhat it supports
0 - 20%Human-likeNo action. Style markers sit in the normal human range.
20 - 75%UnclearNo conclusion either way. Read the passages if you are curious, but this band is not evidence.
75 - 90%Likely AI-assistedWorth a conversation about process and drafts. Not a finding on its own.
90 - 100%Very likely AIStrong signal across most passages. Corroborate with version history and the writer's own account.

Writing signals

What the detector actually measures

Every number in the result comes from one of these, and you can open the diagnostics panel to see the raw values for your own text.

Sentence length variation

Human drafts lurch between short and long sentences. Generated prose tends to settle into a narrow, even rhythm.

Vocabulary variety

The ratio of distinct words to total words, measured inside a fixed window so document length cannot move it.

Words used only once

Specific detail, names and odd word choices produce one-off words. Generic phrasing reuses the same vocabulary.

Transition phrase rate

Dense runs of furthermore, additionally and in conclusion are a recognisable pattern in generated academic text.

Punctuation variety

Semicolons, dashes, parentheses and question marks appear unevenly in human writing and more uniformly in generated writing.

Burstiness and perplexity

The strongest signals need a language model. The interface for them exists, so when you plug one in the detector reports that it used it - and widens the interval when it did not.

Honest limits

What this tool cannot tell you

It is not proof of misconduct

A high index means the writing looks statistically like generated prose. That is a reason to ask questions, never a reason to accuse. Academic decisions need process evidence: drafts, version history and a conversation.

Calibrated, but not on coursework

The shipped profile was fitted on HC3 question-and-answer documents and measured on a held-out half: AUC 0.910, accuracy 84.1%. Student coursework is a different register, so treat those numbers as a ceiling rather than a promise.

It is unfair to formal technical prose

At the likely-AI boundary it wrongly flagged 7.0% of human documents overall and 31.6% of human medical writing. Dense clinical or technical human prose is the worst case, and multilingual writers are disadvantaged by statistical detectors generally.

Short text is unreliable

Measured on held-out data, a single-window document was wrongly flagged 13.6% of the time against 2.3% for two windows and 1.5% for three or more. Below roughly 300 words the interval widens to say so.

Paraphrasing defeats it

A few minutes of manual rewriting moves the style markers out of range. Treat a low score as no information rather than as a clean bill of health.

Similarity checking is separate

The plagiarism side of this project needs a licensed reference corpus to compare against, which cannot ship in a browser page. It runs from the command line against your own index.

FAQ

Questions about AI detection

Is my text uploaded anywhere?

No. The scoring engine is downloaded to your browser and your text is passed to it in memory. Nothing is sent to a server, stored or logged. You can confirm this by reading the source, or by loading the page and then going offline before you paste.

Is this Turnitin?

No, and it is not affiliated with or endorsed by Turnitin or any other vendor. It is an independent open source implementation built to the same report structure - similarity index, AI-writing indicator, per passage detail - so you can see how that kind of number is produced.

Will it match what my institution's detector says?

Probably not exactly. Different detectors use different features, different training data and different thresholds, and none of them publish enough for the numbers to be comparable. Use this to review your own writing, not to predict a grade.

My paper is all my own work but the score is high. Why?

Formal academic register looks like generated text to a style based detector: even sentence lengths, heavy transitions, a controlled vocabulary. That is a weakness of the method, not a finding about you. Open the passage breakdown to see which windows drove it.

Can it detect ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini specifically?

No. It measures statistical properties of the writing, not a watermark or a model fingerprint, so it cannot attribute text to a particular product. Anyone claiming otherwise is overselling.

Why is there no percentage of AI sentences?

Because single sentences are far too short to score reliably. The engine refuses to report below its minimum window size, which is why you get passages of about 150 words instead of a sentence by sentence verdict.

Can I run it myself?

Yes. The whole engine is standard library Python with no runtime dependencies, with a command line interface and a test suite. Clone the repository and run it locally or in CI.

What should I do with a high score on my own draft?

Treat it as an editing prompt. Add specific evidence and examples, vary your sentence lengths, cut stacked transition phrases and make sure the argument reads like your own reasoning.

Check a draft before you submit it

Paste your text, read the band and the passage breakdown, then revise with the flagged windows in front of you.
